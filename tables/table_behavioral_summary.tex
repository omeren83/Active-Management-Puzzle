Joint $\chi^2$: Wald test of the hypothesis's state-dependent terms, whose null is no state dependence: H1, the three rank-segment $\times$ state interactions; H2, the three rank-segment $\times$ margin-debt (or put--call) interactions, with the $D^{\text{SENT}}$ interactions as controls in the discriminant row; H3, the lottery measure and its interaction with $D^{\text{SENT}}$; H4, the four fee $\times$ sentiment terms (degrees of freedom 3, 3, 2 and 4); $p$-values in parentheses. ``Joint test'' reports whether it is rejected at 10\%. A rejection shows that flows depend on the state; it does not by itself identify a psychological cause. Headline $z$: hypothesis-specific statistic (H1: $\delta_{\text{HIGH}}-\delta_{\text{LOW}}$; H2: $\delta_{\text{LOW}}$; H3: lottery $\times$ $D^{\text{SENT}}$; H4: $\eta$) with its one-sided $p$-value in the behaviourally predicted direction. ``Headline direction'' names a direction only when the one-sided $p$-value in that direction is below 0.10 (for the opposite direction, $1-p$); otherwise ``Not significant''. For H2 the predicted sign is negative (disposition), and the opposite sign is the margin-call alternative of Brunnermeier and Pedersen (2009). The ActR2 row of H3 is a rational-null control: activeness is not a lottery feature. Each hypothesis is estimated on its own aligned sample (all regressors of its specifications non-missing), so $N$ differs across hypotheses; the H2 put--call rows use the shorter put--call ratio window. Primary specifications use contemporaneous state variables, fund FE for H1--H3 and Lipper $\times$ year-month FE for H4, with two-way clustering on Ticker and calendar month (Petersen 2009). Coefficients are reported in the underlying tables.
