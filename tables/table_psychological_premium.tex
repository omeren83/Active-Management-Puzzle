The 1-sided $p$ tests Diff $<0$ (a larger premium under high sentiment); stars refer to it: $^{*}\,p<0.10$, $^{**}\,p<0.05$, $^{***}\,p<0.01$. \smallskip \textit{Conversion to alpha basis points.} To express the premium in welfare-relevant units, the rank-to-alpha mapping is calibrated in the rank space of the flow regressions: each month, active funds are sorted into quintiles of their within-Lipper-category trailing 12-month return rank (the $R$ of the H1--H4 regressions; bins of width 0.2) and the equal-weighted portfolios are held for the month. The net-return Carhart alpha spread is $Q5-Q1=2.214\%$ p.a. ($t=2.39$); with a midpoint-to-midpoint rank distance of $0.8$, this implies approximately 277 basis points of annual Carhart alpha per rank-point. Applying this conversion at the middle rank segment, the MAX12 premium is approximately 3 basis points of annual alpha per standard deviation at low-sentiment regimes ($|\text{SP}_{\text{low}}|\times 277\approx 0.011\times 277$) and approximately 37 basis points per standard deviation at high-sentiment regimes ($|\text{SP}_{\text{high}}|\times 277\approx 0.133\times 277$). For comparison, the Jegadeesh--Titman momentum quintile spread (Table~\ref{tab:port_mom_alpha}; $2.278\%$, $t=1.36$) implies 285 basis points per rank point. The quoted basis-point figures should be read as orders of magnitude rather than precise estimates. \smallskip Sample: actively managed funds, \textcite{Evans2010}-corrected panel, no date cap; H3 / activeness subsample per flagged\_funds.xlsx.
